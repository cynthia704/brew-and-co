The Perfect Pour-Over

Mastering the geometry of the v60 dripper and finding the optimal bloom time for single-origin Ethiopian beans.

🫖

There is perhaps no brewing method more rewarding than a pour-over. When done right, it yields a cup that highlights the vibrant, true characteristics of your coffee beans—stripping away oils and sediment to leave behind pure clarity.

The Geometry of the V60

The cone shape of the Hario V60 is designed deliberately. The 60-degree angle ensures water flows to the center, extending the contact time with the grounds. This shape isn't just aesthetic; it’s a tool for full extraction.

"The bloom is the coffee waking up. It's the most crucial 45 seconds of your morning."

Step-by-Step Guide

  1. Rinse your filter: Always pour hot water through your empty paper filter first to remove any papery taste and pre-heat your mug.
  2. The Grind: Aim for a medium-fine grind (similar to sea salt). Too fine, and water pools; too coarse, and the water rushes through without extracting flavor.
  3. The Bloom: Pour just enough water to wet all the grounds. You’ll see it bubble and rise as it releases carbon dioxide. Let this rest for 45 seconds.
  4. The Pour: Slowly pour in concentric circles starting from the center outward. Keep the water level consistent.

Patience is the secret ingredient here. Try this recipe with our single-origin Ethiopian Yirgacheffe this weekend!
